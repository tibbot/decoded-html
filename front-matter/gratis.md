# The Conversation Behind the Book

No book like this is written by one person.

Every chapter began as conversations, lectures, questions, failed explanations, puzzled looks, late-night revisions, and moments when someone asked exactly the question I didn't yet know how to answer.

Those conversations shaped these pages far more than any outline ever could.

My gratitude becomes decoded's final demonstration: no meaningful communication—and certainly no book about communication—is ever the work of a single voice.

God
Every page in this book bears the fingerprints of other people. Behind them all, I acknowledge the One to whom I owe the greatest debt. I thank God for gifts I did not earn, opportunities I did not create, grace that met me repeatedly when my own understanding fell short, and for sustaining me through the long work of bringing this ink to paper. ~~these ideas into the world.~~ I'm positive His contributions were most important.


Shelley
My wife lived with this book long before anyone else could read it. She endured unfinished conversations that wandered into chapter ideas, meals interrupted by "just one note," and the peculiar experience of competing with people who existed only in my head. She never asked me to stop thinking about the book, even when it meant I was sometimes somewhere else while sitting in the same room.

This book argues that communication is built through attention. No one gave this project more attention than my wife—not because she edited every page, but because she made it possible for me to keep writing them.


Erin Thomas
Erin Thomas kept asking the question I least wanted to answer. Every time I thought a chapter was finished, she found the place where I had skipped a step. She possesses the unusual ability to ask a question that sounds completely innocent until you realize it requires rewriting twenty pages. Along the way, she taught me that confusion is often a design problem rather than an intelligence problem.


Margaret Moodian
Margaret Moodian read more drafts than anyone should reasonably be asked to read. Her margin notes were responsible for far more rewriting than I initially believed was necessary. She was almost always right.

She found inconsistencies that I had become completely blind to, usually with the unnerving precision of someone who had actually been paying attention. She also possesses  an uncanny instinct for finding the exact sentence readers will misunderstand first.

Maribeth Daniel
Maribeth proved that editing is less about correcting than negotiating. We spent countless hours debating the finer points of em dashes, capitalization after colons, and whether the next section should go farther—or further. Somewhere between and because of those debates, this book became substantially better. She never accepted "close enough" when "clear enough" was still possible.

Students
My students unknowingly tested almost every idea in this book. When an explanation landed, they moved on. When it didn't, they let me know—sometimes politely.


Readers
Every book waits for its final collaborator. Until someone reads it, its ideas remain unfinished. Thank you for giving these pages the opportunity to become something more than words on paper. If this book causes you to pause before sending one email, asking one question, or making one assumption, it will have done more than I could reasonably ask.









The ideas in these pages have occupied me for years. Now they belong to our conversation.
