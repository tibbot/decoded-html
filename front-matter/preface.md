Communication is one of the first skills we begin to develop and one of the last we truly understand.

We spend years learning how to write, present, persuade, negotiate, explain, apologize, encourage, and decide. Yet the principles that make communication succeed often remain hidden beneath the surface. Messages that appear equally capable can produce remarkably different outcomes, and the reasons are not always obvious.

That mystery is what led to *decoded*.

This book does not begin with definitions in the hope that understanding will follow. It begins with observation. By noticing how communication behaves, patterns begin to emerge. Once those patterns become familiar, giving them a name feels less like memorization and more like recognition.

I have long believed that the things we discover become our own. The things we are merely told are often borrowed just long enough to pass a quiz.

If *decoded* succeeds, it will not simply leave you with new terminology. It will leave you seeing communication differently. And once you've seen it, it becomes difficult not to see it everywhere.
